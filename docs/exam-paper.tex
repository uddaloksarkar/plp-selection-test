%================================================================== Q1
\item \textbf{Disk full on the lab file server.} \hfill [14 marks]

\emph{Reported by Debarshi, research scholar:} ``I cannot copy anything into
\texttt{/srv/data}, it says the disk is full, and nothing I can see in there
accounts for that much space. Submitting a job into \texttt{/srv/spool}
\emph{also} says the disk is full, but \texttt{df -h} shows that one has plenty
of space left. That part makes no sense to me.''

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Background: this server has three separate disks.}
\texttt{/srv/data} and \texttt{/srv/spool} look like ordinary folders, but each
one is really its own small disk, attached at that path (a \emph{mount point}).
The rest of the system lives on a third, much larger disk.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l r@{}}
/          & the system disk         & about 24 GB \\
/srv/data  & the project data disk   & 1 GB \\
/srv/spool & the job-submission disk & 48 MB \\
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

Each disk fills up on its own: one being full says nothing about the others.
\verb|df -h / /srv/data /srv/spool| prints one line per disk.
\end{framed}

Though the two complaints look alike, they are \textbf{two unrelated faults on two different disks}, with different causes. Fixing one does nothing for the other. 

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q1(a)
\hd{(a)\enspace \texttt{/srv/data} is full \hfill [11 marks]}
\texttt{/srv/data} is at 100\%, and the files you can see there do not add up
to the space reported as used.

\textit{Reproduce:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/data                        # 100% used
sudo du -sh /srv/data                  # much smaller than df says is in use
\end{shell}

\textit{Fix:} bring \texttt{/srv/data} back to \textbf{under 10\% used} (the
\texttt{Use\%} column of \texttt{df -h}). There is more than one reason this
disk is full: freeing the largest obvious files will not finish the job. Consider the differences shown in the output of \texttt{df} and \texttt{du}. Explain the reason for the difference.

\textit{Check:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/data                       # Use% under 10%
ls /srv/data/current                  # five survey-block CSV files, still there
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q1(b)
\hd{(b)\enspace \texttt{/srv/spool} refuses new files \hfill [3 marks]}
New files cannot be created on \texttt{/srv/spool}, \emph{although
\texttt{df -h} reports free space on it}. Something other than free space has
run out. This has nothing to do with \texttt{/srv/data}.

\textit{Reproduce:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/spool                       # shows space free
touch /srv/spool/incoming/testjob      # "No space left on device"
\end{shell}

\textit{Fix:} make \texttt{/srv/spool} able to accept new files again. It is
\emph{not} short of space: freeing bytes there will not help. A filesystem can
run out of more than one kind of resource, and \texttt{df} has another mode
that reports the other one. Explain why \texttt{/srv/spool} reported ``No space left on device'' while \texttt{df -h} showed free space.

\textit{Check:}
\begin{shell}
touch /srv/spool/incoming/t && echo OK && rm /srv/spool/incoming/t
\end{shell}

\hd{Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Nothing under \texttt{/srv/data/current} may be deleted or changed.
        It is live survey data with no second copy on this machine.
  \item \texttt{/srv/data/archive/README.txt} says what is safe to remove
        there. Read it before deleting anything.
\end{itemize}

%================================================================== Q2
\item \textbf{Shared project folder unusable.} \hfill [16 marks]

\emph{Reported for the ACMU Lab group:} ``Buddhadev cannot save anything into
\texttt{shared/}--- he could last
month. And Chandrima from the internal audit cell needs to \emph{read} that
folder for the annual review, and cannot see anything at all.''

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Background: who is who.} \texttt{/srv/projects/acmu/} (and
\texttt{shared/} inside it) belongs to the \texttt{acmu} group. Anyone who is
neither a member nor the auditor should have no access at all.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l l@{}}
arnab     & member of \texttt{acmu}        & read and write everything \\
buddhadev & member of \texttt{acmu}        & read and write everything \\
chandrima & auditor, \textbf{not} a member & read everything, write nothing \\
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

To see the current state:
\begin{shell}
cd /srv/projects/acmu
stat -c '%a %U:%G %n' . shared shared/notes.md
id buddhadev
getfacl /srv/projects/acmu/shared
\end{shell}
\end{framed}

Underneath the two complaints are \textbf{three independent faults}, each with
its own cause and fix, each marked on its own. Take them in any order.

\textbf{Test as the users named, not as root.} Root can do everything, so a
check run as root proves nothing.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(a)
\hd{(a)\enspace Buddhadev is no longer in the \texttt{acmu} group \hfill [5 marks]}
\textit{Reproduce:} Check \verb|id buddhadev| where \texttt{acmu} is missing.
\textit{Fix:} put him back, without disturbing his other groups. Keep checking with \verb|id buddhadev| until it shows \texttt{acmu} again.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(b)
\hd{(b)\enspace The shared folder does not let the group write \hfill [8 marks]}
Members of \texttt{acmu} cannot create files in the folder at all. This is
about the permission bits on the two directories, \texttt{/srv/projects/acmu}
and \texttt{shared/}. Arnab is still a member, so test as arnab.
\begin{shell}
sudo -u arnab touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1     # Permission denied
stat -c '%a %U:%G %n' /srv/projects/acmu /srv/projects/acmu/shared
\end{shell}
\textit{Fix:} both directories must let the \texttt{acmu} group read, write and
enter them, and give everyone else no write access. 

\begin{shell}
sudo -u arnab touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1 && echo "write OK"
sudo rm -f /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(c)
\hd{(c)\enspace The auditor cannot read the folder \hfill [3 marks]}
\textit{Fix:} grant read-only access to everything under
\texttt{/srv/projects/acmu/}, including files created there in future, and do
it \emph{without} adding chandrima to the group.
\begin{shell}
sudo -u chandrima cat /srv/projects/acmu/shared/notes.md   # must work
sudo -u chandrima touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t2       # must FAIL
\end{shell}

\hd{When all three are done}
Buddhadev's complaint needs (a) and (b) together:
\begin{shell}
sudo -u buddhadev touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t3        # must work
sudo -u buddhadev cat /srv/projects/acmu/shared/notes.md    # must work
\end{shell}

\hd{Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Chandrima must not be put into the \texttt{acmu} group.}
        Audit policy forbids putting auditors into the groups they audit:
        read everything, write nothing.
  \item No \texttt{chmod 777}, anywhere. This is a multi-user machine.
\end{itemize}


%================================================================== Q3
\item \textbf{Cannot log in to the ACMU lab server.} \hfill [10 marks]

\emph{Reported by Debarshi, research scholar:} ``I am supposed to log in to
the lab server as \texttt{acmusrv}. It worked last week. Now it just says
\emph{Permission denied}, and then asks me for a password I was never given. I
have not touched anything since.''

\texttt{ssh acmulab} should log you in to the account \texttt{acmusrv} on the
lab server using the key \verb|~/.ssh/id_acmu|, \textbf{without a password}.
Two things are wrong, and they produce the \textbf{same message}:
\texttt{Permission denied (publickey)}. Identify both of them. 

\hd{How to reproduce it}
\begin{shell}
ssh acmulab

ssh -v acmulab 2>&1 | grep -i 'identity file\|offering\|publickey'
cat ~/.ssh/config
ls -l ~/.ssh/
\end{shell}

\hd{What to fix \hfill [10 marks]}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic{enumi}.\arabic*},leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Make the \texttt{acmulab} alias use the right key.} 
  The \verb|~/.ssh/config| file is corrupted. Fix it so that \texttt{ssh acmulab} succeeds without a password prompt.

  \item \textbf{Make the key usable.} 
  Find out why ssh still refuses to use the key, and fix it.
\end{enumerate}

\hd{How to check you have fixed it}
\begin{shell}
ssh -o BatchMode=yes acmulab whoami     # prints: acmusrv
ls -l ~/.ssh/id_acmu                    # -rw------- (0600)
\end{shell}
\texttt{BatchMode=yes} forbids any password prompt, so if this prints
\texttt{acmusrv} the key really is doing the work.


\end{enumerate}
