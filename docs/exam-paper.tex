%================================================================== Q1
\item \textbf{Disk full on the lab file server.} \hfill [14 marks]

\emph{Reported by Debarshi Roy, research scholar:} ``I cannot copy anything into
\texttt{/srv/data} --- it says the disk is full, and nothing I can see in there
accounts for that much space. Submitting a job into \texttt{/srv/spool}
\emph{also} says the disk is full, but \texttt{df -h} shows that one has plenty
of space left. That part makes no sense to me.''

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Background: this server has three separate disks.}
\texttt{/srv/data} and \texttt{/srv/spool} look like ordinary folders, but each
one is really its own small disk, attached at that path (a \emph{mount point}).
The rest of the system lives on a third, much larger disk.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l r@{}}
/          & the system disk         & about 24 GB \\
/srv/data  & the project data disk   & 1 GB \\
/srv/spool & the job-submission disk & 48 MB \\
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

Each disk fills up on its own: one being full says nothing about the others.
\verb|df -h / /srv/data /srv/spool| prints one line per disk.
\end{framed}

The two complaints look alike --- both end in ``No space left on device'' ---
but they are \textbf{two unrelated faults on two different disks}, with
different causes. Fixing one does nothing for the other. Tackle them in either
order; each is marked on its own.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q1(a)
\hd{(a)\enspace \texttt{/srv/data} is full \hfill [11 marks]}
\texttt{/srv/data} is at 100\%, and the files you can see there do not add up
to the space reported as used.

\textit{Reproduce:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/data                        # 100% used
sudo du -sh /srv/data                  # much smaller than df says is in use
\end{shell}

\textit{Fix:} bring \texttt{/srv/data} back to \textbf{under 10\% used} (the
\texttt{Use\%} column of \texttt{df -h}). There is more than one reason this
disk is full: freeing the largest obvious files will not get you there on its
own. Treat any difference between what \texttt{df} says is used and what
\texttt{du} can account for as something you still have to explain.

\textit{Check:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/data                       # Use% under 10%
ls /srv/data/current                  # five survey-block CSV files, still there
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q1(b)
\hd{(b)\enspace \texttt{/srv/spool} refuses new files \hfill [3 marks]}
New files cannot be created on \texttt{/srv/spool}, \emph{although
\texttt{df -h} reports free space on it}. Something other than free space has
run out. This has nothing to do with \texttt{/srv/data}.

\textit{Reproduce:}
\begin{shell}
df -h /srv/spool                       # shows space free
touch /srv/spool/incoming/testjob      # "No space left on device"
\end{shell}

\textit{Fix:} make \texttt{/srv/spool} able to accept new files again. It is
\emph{not} short of space: freeing bytes there will not help. A filesystem can
run out of more than one kind of resource, and \texttt{df} has another mode
that reports the other one. Then, in \verb|~/FIXLOG.md|, explain in two or
three sentences why \texttt{/srv/spool} reported ``No space left on device''
while \texttt{df -h} showed free space.

\textit{Check:}
\begin{shell}
touch /srv/spool/incoming/t && echo OK && rm /srv/spool/incoming/t
\end{shell}

\hd{Constraints --- these apply to both parts, and are marked}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Nothing under \texttt{/srv/data/current} may be deleted or changed.
        It is live survey data with no second copy on this machine.
  \item Do not reformat, recreate, resize or unmount either disk.
  \item \texttt{/srv/data/archive/README.txt} says what is safe to remove
        there. Read it before deleting anything.
  \item Rebooting clears one of these faults. On a real file server at 11am
        you would not have that option; treat it as unavailable.
\end{itemize}

%================================================================== Q2
\item \textbf{Shared project folder unusable.} \hfill [16 marks]

\emph{Reported for the ACMU Lab group:} ``Buddhadev cannot save anything
into \texttt{shared/}, and cannot open \texttt{notes.md} at all. When I create
a file there, the others cannot edit it --- it comes out belonging to my own
personal group instead of the project group.
\texttt{sudo systemctl restart reportd} used to work for all of us; now it is
refused. And Chandrima from the internal audit cell needs to \emph{read} that
folder for the annual review. He cannot see anything.''

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Background: who is who.} \texttt{/srv/projects/acmu/} (and
\texttt{shared/} inside it) belongs to the \texttt{acmu} group. Anyone who
is not a member or the auditor should have no access at all.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\ttfamily}l l l@{}}
arnab   & member of \texttt{acmu} & read and write everything \\
buddhadev  & member of \texttt{acmu} & read and write everything \\
chandrima & auditor, \textbf{not} a member & read everything, write nothing \\
\end{tabular}
\smallskip

To see the current state:
\begin{shell}
cd /srv/projects/acmu
stat -c '%a %U:%G %n' . shared shared/notes.md
id buddhadev; getfacl shared; sudo cat /etc/sudoers.d/acmu
\end{shell}
\end{framed}

The complaints overlap, but underneath them are \textbf{five independent
faults}, each with its own cause and fix. Fixing one does nothing for the
others; each is marked on its own. Tackle them in any order. Test as the users
named, not as root --- root can do everything, so a check run as root proves
nothing.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(a)
\hd{(a)\enspace Buddhadev is no longer in the \texttt{acmu} group \hfill [2 marks]}
\textit{Reproduce:} \verb|id buddhadev| --- \texttt{acmu} is missing.
\textit{Fix:} put him back, without changing any of his other groups.
\begin{shell}
id -nG buddhadev | grep -qw acmu && echo "buddhadev is a member"
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(b)
\hd{(b)\enspace The shared folder: group write, and which group new files get \hfill [8 marks]}
Members cannot create files in the folder, and once they can, new files come
out in the creator's \emph{personal} group instead of \texttt{acmu}. Both
are about the permission bits on the two directories
\texttt{/srv/projects/acmu} and \texttt{shared/}. Arnab is still a member,
so test as her.

\textit{Fix:} both directories let the group read and write, give everyone
else no access, and make new files inside them belong to \texttt{acmu}
automatically. A directory can carry more than the nine permission bits you see
first.
\begin{shell}
sudo -u arnab touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1 && echo "write OK"
stat -c '%U:%G' /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1     # group must be: acmu
sudo rm -f /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t1
\end{shell}
The \texttt{stat} line is the one people miss: if the group comes out as
\texttt{arnab}, this part is not fixed yet.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(c)
\hd{(c)\enspace \texttt{notes.md} has become private \hfill [2 marks]}
\texttt{shared/notes.md} is the group's working file, but now belongs to one
person and only she can read it. \textit{Fix:} group \texttt{acmu},
readable and writable by the group, no access for anyone else.
\begin{shell}
stat -c '%a %G' /srv/projects/acmu/shared/notes.md    # 660 acmu
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(d)
\hd{(d)\enspace The group's \texttt{sudo} right is broken \hfill [3 marks]}
Members of \texttt{acmu} --- and only them --- should be able to run
\texttt{sudo -n systemctl restart reportd} without a password. The rule is in
\texttt{/etc/sudoers.d/acmu} and no longer does what it was meant to.
\textit{Fix:} correct the rule, editing the file only with
\texttt{sudo visudo -f /etc/sudoers.d/acmu} --- never a plain editor.
\begin{shell}
sudo -u arnab sudo -n systemctl restart reportd && echo "sudo OK"
sudo visudo -cf /etc/sudoers.d/acmu                   # parsed OK
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q2(e)
\hd{(e)\enspace The auditor cannot read the folder \hfill [1 mark]}
Chandrima's access came from per-user entries on the files themselves, and those
have been wiped. \textit{Fix:} give him read-only access to everything under
\texttt{/srv/projects/acmu/}, including files created there in future ---
\emph{without} adding him to the group.
\begin{shell}
sudo -u chandrima cat /srv/projects/acmu/shared/notes.md   # must work
sudo -u chandrima touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t2       # must FAIL
\end{shell}

\hd{When all five are done}
Buddhadev's original complaint needs (a), (b) and (c) together:
\begin{shell}
sudo -u buddhadev touch /srv/projects/acmu/shared/t3        # must work
sudo -u buddhadev cat /srv/projects/acmu/shared/notes.md    # must work
\end{shell}

\hd{Constraints --- these apply to every part, and are marked}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Chandrima must not be put into the \texttt{acmu} group.}
        Audit policy forbids putting auditors into the groups they audit. He
        must be able to read and must \emph{not} be able to write.
  \item No \texttt{chmod 777}, anywhere. This is a multi-user machine.
  \item Do not change anyone's login shell, password or home directory.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Hint.} Group membership changes do not affect a shell that is already
open; \texttt{sudo -u} \textit{user} starts a fresh one.

%================================================================== Q3
\item \textbf{The lab dashboard nobody can reach.} \hfill [10 marks]

\emph{Reported by the ACMU Lab:} ``Our results dashboard has been down
since Friday's maintenance. We open \texttt{http://gateway:8080} and get
nothing. Somebody said the tunnel service on labbox keeps restarting. We need
it back before the review meeting.''

\begin{framed}
\textbf{Background: how the dashboard gets out of the basement.} The dashboard
runs on \textbf{labbox}, a basement machine. Campus IT lets labbox connect
\emph{out}, but nothing may connect \emph{in}. So labbox publishes it through
\textbf{gateway} (this VM) with a \emph{reverse SSH tunnel}:
\begin{shell}
colleagues --> gateway:8080 ==== SSH tunnel ====> labbox --> 127.0.0.1:8888
              (this VM)    <-- labbox dials OUT --          (the dashboard)
\end{shell}
labbox runs, as the service \texttt{lab-tunnel}:
\begin{shell}
ssh -N -R 0.0.0.0:8080:localhost:8888 tunnel@gateway
\end{shell}

In \texttt{-R}, the first half (\texttt{0.0.0.0:8080}) is where
\emph{gateway} listens. The second half (\texttt{localhost:8888}) is where
labbox delivers each connection, \emph{as labbox sees it} --- so that
\texttt{localhost} is labbox's own.

\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l l@{}}
\textbf{gateway} & \texttt{10.20.0.1}, this VM & you are already here \\
\textbf{labbox}  & \texttt{10.20.0.2}, basement & \texttt{sudo labbox} opens its console \\
\end{tabular}

\smallskip
labbox is simulated on this VM and shares its files; its own are under
\texttt{/opt/labbox/}. Its services are \texttt{labbox-dashboard} and
\texttt{lab-tunnel}.
\end{framed}

Friday's maintenance broke the tunnel in \textbf{two independent places}.
Each has its own cause and fix and is marked on its own; fixing one does
nothing for the others. Tackle them in any order --- though you will only
\emph{see} (b) in action once (a) lets the tunnel log in.

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q3(a)
\hd{(a)\enspace gateway refuses labbox's key \hfill [5 marks]}
labbox logs in to gateway as \texttt{tunnel}, with a key and no password.
gateway is rejecting it, so the tunnel never comes up and restarts every few
seconds. The client's log says \emph{that} it was denied; the server's says
\emph{why}.
\begin{shell}
journalctl -u lab-tunnel -n 5          # Permission denied (publickey...)
journalctl -u ssh -n 20                # gateway's side of the story
\end{shell}
\textit{Fix:} make gateway accept the key again. The key itself is correct;
sshd refuses an \texttt{authorized\_keys} file that others could have tampered
with.
\begin{shell}
journalctl -u ssh -n 5                 # Accepted publickey for tunnel
\end{shell}

%------------------------------------------------------------------ Q3(b)
\hd{(b)\enspace The tunnel leads to the wrong place \hfill [5 marks]}
The \texttt{-R} setting decides where on labbox each connection ends up. It
must lead to the dashboard.
\begin{shell}
sudo labbox ss -tlnp                   # what is really listening on labbox
systemctl cat lab-tunnel               # where the tunnel sends connections
\end{shell}
\textit{Fix:} correct the tunnel in
\texttt{/etc/systemd/system/lab-tunnel.service}, then
\texttt{systemctl daemon-reload} and \texttt{systemctl restart lab-tunnel}.
\begin{shell}
systemctl cat lab-tunnel | grep -- -R  # ends in localhost:8888
\end{shell}

\hd{When both are done}
\begin{shell}
curl -s http://10.20.0.1:8080 | grep ACMU   # ACMU-DASHBOARD-OK
\end{shell}
You can also open \texttt{http://192.168.56.10:8080} in a browser on your own
machine --- that is what the colleagues see.

\hd{Constraints --- these apply to every part, and are marked}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{Do not turn off \texttt{StrictModes}} (or any other sshd safety
        check) to make the key work. Fix the file.
  \item \textbf{labbox must stay unreachable.} Do not loosen its firewall or
        move the dashboard off \texttt{127.0.0.1}: going round the tunnel is
        not fixing it.
  \item Do not give the \texttt{tunnel} account a password or a login shell.
  \item Always run \texttt{sudo sshd -t} before reloading sshd. A broken sshd
        configuration can lock everyone out, you included.
\end{itemize}

\end{enumerate}
